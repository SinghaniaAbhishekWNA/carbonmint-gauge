Gauge reader v1 service report: what we built and how it differs from v0

Technical edition · Oct 13, 2026 · engine 1.0.0, commit d510b11 · report schema 1.0

v1 reads the water level of a paddy-field gauge from a drone photo. It runs as a Docker container: a zip of photos goes into one folder and a JSON report comes out of another. On the 127-photo pilot batch, v1 gives an accepted, exactly correct stripe count on 97 photos (76.4%) against 19 (15.0%) for the existing reader, v0. The target is 70–80%. This figure comes from the same photos v1 was developed on; a fresh, unseen batch is needed before it can be called validated accuracy.

1. What we did

The client flies a drone over each paddy field every day and photographs a float gauge in the water. Red stripes come up out of a black sleeve as the water rises, about one stripe per centimetre; no stripes means the field is dry. Crop often grows over the gauge, and the client's existing reader (v0) gets only about half of the photos right. We were asked to reach 70–80% without changing the gauge.

  1. Answer key. Every pilot photo has a hand count of its stripes (section 6 describes how it was made and checked).
  2. v0 rebuilt and measured. v0's code was not available, so it was rebuilt rule by rule from its written description and run on the same photos.
  3. v1 designed. v1 measures the stripe rhythm instead of counting bars, then a small trained model estimates how likely the count is to be exactly right, and the client's acceptance rule decides. Each idea was tried separately and kept only if it helped on fields the model had not seen.
  4. Weak spots checked. Enlarging far gauges, adding a stripe at the sleeve and other ideas were tried and dropped because they did not help (section 7).
  5. Packaged as a service and tested with the real zip (127 photos, 713 MB): the container gives the research code's answer on every photo.

When a zip is readEvery POLL_SECONDS the watcher lists in/*.zip. A zip is read only when its size is non-zero and the same as on the previous look, so a copy in progress is never read.
Fingerprint and duplicatesSHA-256 of the zip. Fingerprints of read zips are kept in out/.processed.json and survive restarts. A zip with a known fingerprint, under any name, is moved to in/done without a new report.
VisitsPhotos are grouped by their folder. A folder whose name matches <checkpoint> - <12 digits> and holds 1–4 photos is one visit. Loose photos, and folders with more than 4 photos, are read one photo per visit (flag no_twin_shot).
Files skippedNames with an absolute path, a drive letter or .. (reason unsafe path); anything not jpg/jpeg/png (not a jpg or png); a photo larger than MAX_PHOTO_MB uncompressed (photo too large). All are listed under skipped.
Zip-level failureNot a readable zip, no jpg/png inside, or more than MAX_IMAGES photos: out/<zip>.error.json is written and the zip goes to in/failed.
Photo-level failureA photo that cannot be decoded or read from the zip gets status: error; the rest of the batch carries on.
WritingReports and error files are written to a .part file and renamed, so a half-written report is never visible.
Health checkThe watcher writes a heartbeat at every poll and after every visit. python -m gaugesvc health passes while the heartbeat is younger than 3 × POLL_SECONDS + 120 s (150 s by default). Docker runs it every 30 s (timeout 10 s, start period 30 s, 3 retries).

4. How one photo is read

StepExact rule
Stripe pixelsR − max(G, B) > 25, R > 1.25 × max(G, B), R > 40 and B ≥ G − 12 (straw and brown soil, where G is well above B, are excluded). Red is judged against green and blue, not by brightness, so shaded stripes count.
Find the rodStripe blobs of ≥ 8 px are grouped vertically; a group needs ≥ 3 separate stripes on a near-vertical axis (|directiony| ≥ 0.85, elongated). Pieces split by a leaf are joined when they lie within 2.5 half-widths of the same axis. Largest score wins. With no stripe stack, a tall, thin, near-vertical blue-grey tube (fill ≥ 0.3, length ≥ 3 widths) is taken instead.
StraightenThe ring centre is found row by row in a strip 5 half-widths wide; a quadratic (straight line for short rods) through those centres follows lean and bend, and the rod's core is resampled along it.
No paint showingFewer than 4 rows with stripe paint: the reading is 0 stripes (dry), a valid reading.
Stripe gap (pitch)Autocorrelation of the redness down the rod. The pitch is the shortest lag whose peak is at least 0.75 of the strongest, so a stretch of 2–4 stripes is not taken as one gap. With 2–6 clear stripes the median spacing is used instead.
End stripesPeaks at either end weaker than 0.3 × the median stripe are dropped (sleeve rims, stains).
CountGap by gap from the first stripe to the last: each gap counts round(gap ÷ local pitch) stripes, at least 1, where the local pitch is the median of the neighbouring gaps that lie between 0.6 and 1.5 × the pitch. Hidden stripes inside the run are counted; perspective is followed.
Colour locatorOnly when nothing above finds a rod: our colour method's locator (brightness evened out first, then plain) finds the gauge by red stripes, blue-grey pipe and black sleeve on a 1000 px copy, and counts bands in its box (no bands = dry). Its reading enters the model with a fixed confidence input of 0.3.
ConfidenceLogistic regression (L2 = 1, fitted by Newton steps) on 10 standardised inputs: the reader's own score, pattern strength, share of stripes actually seen, log sharpness, log rod length, red coverage, the other shot reads the same count, the other shot within 1, no other shot, reading is 0. Output: P(count is exactly right).
The other shotThe two shots of a visit are read first; each shot's features include the other's count. A missing or unreadable twin is an input (“no other shot”) and a flag, not a failure.
Bad gaugeMore than 25 stripes, or a stripe gap outside 0.45–1.7 × the rod's half-width (with ≥ 3 stripes): never accepted, flag bad_gauge, confidence capped below 0.7.
AcceptP ≥ 0.7 (the client's bar). Visit reading = mean of its accepted shots (the client's folder rule); none accepted → the visit stays in review.

6. Evaluation method

Dataset

127 drone photos (4096 × 3072 JPEG, about 5.6 MB each) from 7 fields, 66 visits, 23 Sep – 6 Oct 2026; the client's pilot batch minus one photo missing from our copy. This is the development dataset: the reading rules were designed while looking at these photos.

Ground truth (data/labels.csv)

  1. For every photo the gauge was straightened and enlarged (scripts/label_strips.py) and the first and last visible stripe marked. The stripes were counted as whole stripes; a stripe partly hidden by the cap or sleeve counts when it is mostly visible. The first read was made by an AI assistant from these enlarged strips. Each label carries a confidence (high 103, low 24) and an occlusion flag (46 photos).
  2. A person checked random samples against the photos (gauge/spotcheck.py, fixed seed). Round 1, 15 photos: 10 exact, mostly a half-stripe convention; the convention was fixed to whole stripes and 3 labels corrected. Round 2, 15 new photos: 15 of 15 exact.

Photo sets

Each photo was sorted by eye into one set: Clear (59), the gauge and its stripes plainly visible; Partly hidden (43), leaves or grass across the stripes; Hard (25), the gauge barely visible even to the eye.

Metrics

Accepted and exact (primary)Photos with status accepted whose stripe count equals the label, divided by all 127 photos
Accepted but wrongAccepted photos whose count differs from the label by any amount
Right when acceptedAccepted and exact ÷ accepted
Review, no gauge, errorShares of photos with those statuses
VisitsA visit counts when the mean of its accepted shots is within 0.5 of the visit's median label

Leave one field out. The confidence model is scored field by field: for each of the 7 fields it is trained on the other 6 and applied to that field, so no photo is scored by a model that saw its field. The figures below use this. The deployed service uses one model trained on all 7 fields, so on these same photos it looks slightly better (111 accepted, 98 exact); that in-sample figure is not quoted as accuracy. Reproduce: python -m gauge.run, then python scripts/compare.py (writes docs/comparison.md).

The 76.4% is measured on the development dataset. It shows the method works on these fields; it is not independent validation. A fresh labelled batch from fields and days v1 has never seen is needed to estimate real-world accuracy.

Under the looser rule used in some pilot reports (±1, or ±3 on estimated tall stems; 9 uncountable photos left out), v1 scores 94 of 118 (79.7%); see the companion note.

10. Testing and reproducibility

  • Parity with the research code: python service/tests/parity.py service/out/waypoints.report.json → same reading 127/127, same accept decision 127/127.
  • Service tests (python -m unittest discover -s service/tests, 8 tests): visit grouping, damaged photo, hostile names (../, drive letters), non-photo files, loose photos, folders of more than 4, empty / photo-less / broken / oversized zips, soil rules, visit rule, report validated against the schema, partial copy, duplicate under another name, broken zip to failed with its fingerprint, fingerprints kept across restarts.
  • Reader tests (python -m unittest tests/test_gauge.py tests/test_app.py, 31 tests): synthetic gauges with known answers, hidden stripes, a pitch that the old rule took at 4× the true gap, dry gauges, the confidence model, the bad-gauge rule, the colour-locator fallback.
  • Live checks: real zip end to end twice; the same zip dropped again under another name was recognised and skipped; container healthy, non-root, restart policy set.
  • Versions: commit d510b11; Python 3.11.17 in the image; packages pinned in service/requirements.txt; ground truth data/labels.csv; leave-one-field-out results data/v2_reads.csv.
