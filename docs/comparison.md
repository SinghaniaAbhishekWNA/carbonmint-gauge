Notes:
- **v0** is rebuilt in Python from the company's written description of `src/enhanced-detector.ts`; numbers the description leaves open are marked ASSUMED in `gauge/v0.py`. Its ~50% comes from the company's own photos; on these photos it scores lower. Diagnostic (`scripts/v0_diag.py`): its colour rule needs value >= 50%, but shaded rings here sit at about 25-40%; relaxing only that rule lifts it from 19 to 55 accepted-and-exact. The magenta hue rule (140-175) changes nothing on these photos.
- **Teammate** has no confidence on its ring count; 'accepted' = its locator's own confident states (score >= 0.30), not the company's 0.7.
- **Our reader, own score**: same readings as v1, but its untrained confidence rarely reaches 0.7.
- **v1** confidence is a logistic model trained **leave-one-plot-out**: each plot's photos are scored by a model that never saw that plot. Accept = probability >= 0.7. Bad-gauge check: 1 of 127 correct-gauge photos falsely flagged.
- **v2** = v1 plus two reader fixes and the teammate's locator: (1) the ring gap is the shortest autocorrelation lag at least 0.75 as strong as the best (the best can be 2-4x the true gap); (2) rings are counted gap by gap against the local gap, since perspective changes the gap along the rod; (3) when our rod finder sees nothing, the teammate's colour locator is used (local lane only: needs OpenCV). Same features, same leave-one-plot-out training. Tried and dropped (no gain or more errors on these photos): Lanczos 2-3x upscaling of small rods, an extra ring at the sleeve, extending the count along a faint rhythm, extra confidence signals, and the teammate's count / box / score as confidence signals.
